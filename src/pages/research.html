Research

The crystal serves the physics: we design and grow large, high-quality single crystals of interesting quantum materials in order to make interesting measurements possible. Four threads run through the group's work.

Emergent transport in correlated and magnetic materials

We investigate how correlated electrons and magnetic order give rise to unconventional transport phenomena in crystalline materials. We focus on systems where interactions between electronic degrees of freedom (charge, spin, and orbital), an underlying crystal lattice, and symmetry lead to behaviors that defy classical expectations, such as anomalous Hall effects, nonlinear responses, and transport signatures of topological or frustrated states. These phenomena often emerge near phase boundaries or in materials with complex magnetic ordering, geometric frustration, or broken inversion symmetry.

To access and tune these states, we employ a range of synthesis strategies to produce high-quality powders, single crystals, and compositionally controlled materials. Combining structural and chemical design with low-temperature transport measurements lets us connect microscopic interactions to macroscopic electronic behavior, with relevance to spintronics and quantum technologies.

Quantum magnetism and frustration

We study materials in which magnetic interactions are inherently frustrated: the magnetic moments cannot reach a long-range ordered state due to geometric or exchange constraints. Frustration can give rise to exotic ground states such as quantum spin liquids, spin ices, and dynamically fluctuating disordered states. These systems sit at the forefront of quantum materials research, offering a platform to study entanglement, fractionalized excitations, and emergent gauge behavior in real materials.

Our group synthesizes and characterizes magnetic materials with frustrated lattice geometries and low-dimensional connectivity. Through low-temperature magnetometry, thermodynamic measurements, and structural analysis, we look for the organizing principles behind quantum magnetic behavior, in both well-known and chemically underexplored lattice types.

Superconductivity and competing electronic orders

We investigate materials in which superconductivity arises alongside, or in competition with, other electronic orders such as magnetism, charge density waves, or orbital instabilities. These intertwined phenomena produce complex phase diagrams, where subtle shifts in composition, structure, or dimensionality can tip the balance between ground states.

Rather than aiming to solve the mechanism of high-temperature superconductivity outright, we synthesize and characterize crystalline materials that exhibit superconductivity near magnetic or electronically ordered phases, identifying and tuning the structural and electronic parameters that influence phase stability and instability.

Autonomous crystal growth: AutoFlux

Flux crystal growth remains an artisanal craft. Outcomes hinge on experience and intuition, growths are difficult to reproduce between laboratories, and what actually happens inside the crucible is opaque: the primary literature records recipes, not processes. AutoFlux is the group's answer, an automated platform for in situ monitoring and optimization of flux crystal growth.

The long-term vision is to close the loop between observation and control, making flux growth reproducible, documentable, and ultimately learnable by machines as well as people. The platform is under active development in our dedicated growth laboratory, with ZnWO4 grown from NaCl flux serving as a model proof-of-concept system.
