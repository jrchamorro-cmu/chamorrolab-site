Software

Running a synthesis lab generates a great deal of operational knowledge that usually stays trapped in one lab, or in one person's head: which furnace runs hot, when a gas cylinder will run out, what thermal history produced the good crystal. We build software to capture it, and we share what we build.

Maestro

Open-source lab instrument orchestrator · github.com/jrchamorro-cmu/maestro

A solid state chemistry lab is full of instruments that do not talk to each other. Furnaces run multi-day heating sequences with no record beyond a notebook entry. Gas cylinders empty without warning. Chemical inventory lives in a spreadsheet nobody updates. Maestro connects all of it into one system: every instrument reachable from a web dashboard on any lab computer, every reading logged centrally.

Furnaces

Drives the whole furnace fleet through off-the-shelf controllers: multi-step temperature sequences (ramp, dwell, controlled cooling), software safety limits, a claim system so two people never program the same furnace, per-run records, and health monitoring.

Why it matters

Crystal growth lives and dies on thermal history. Growths run unattended for days, and the difference between a powder and a beautiful single crystal can be cooling at 0.1 °C/hr instead of 1 °C/hr. With every run recorded automatically, the growth that worked can be traced and repeated exactly — and a furnace that starts drifting is caught before it costs a sample.

Gas sensors

Wireless pressure sensors on gas cylinders and an air quality monitor, read over Bluetooth, with full history and an overnight leak check.

Why it matters

Furnace runs consume gas continuously for days, and a cylinder that quietly empties mid-run ruins the atmosphere — and the sample — without anyone noticing. Pressure trends forecast when a swap is due before it becomes an emergency, the leak check protects both people and expensive gas, and nobody walks to the lab at 11 pm just to read a gauge.

Inventory

The lab's chemical inventory and safety data sheets, searchable from the same dashboard as everything else.

Why it matters

Planning a synthesis starts with knowing what is actually on the shelf. A live inventory means no duplicate orders and no discovering mid-week that a precursor ran out, and having the SDS one click away at the point of use turns safety compliance from a chore into a habit.

Design principles
IsolationOne module drives real heat. Each module is a separate process, so a Bluetooth glitch or an inventory bug can never take furnace control down with it.
Commodity hardwareNo proprietary DAQ, no custom electronics: off-the-shelf furnace controllers, inexpensive wireless sensors, and one ordinary computer.
Data firstEvery reading and every run is logged centrally. The lab's operational history becomes something you can search and analyze, not something you have to remember.
